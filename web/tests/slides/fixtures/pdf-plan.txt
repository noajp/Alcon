I. Why We Need a Plan
1. Where We Fell Short
Commute times in Harbor City have grown for a decade, and the bus network still
follows routes drawn in 1987. Past plans were wish lists that never said what we
would stop doing.
A plan must choose. Not every corridor can be first, and the purpose of this plan is
to move the most people in the least time for the money we have.2
II. What We Want
We want a city where most trips take under 30 minutes. Average commute time has
risen from 34 minutes in 2015 to 41 minutes today, and the share of trips by bus
fell from 22 percent to 15 percent.
Our goals for the next decade are:
•
•
•
Frequent buses on the six busiest corridors;
Safe, connected bike lanes in every district;
Streets that put people before parking; and
•
Fares that working families can afford.
The goal of this plan is to make the bus the fastest way across town.3
III. How We Get There
1. Principles
We will be guided by a few simple principles:
• Frequency First – A bus every ten minutes beats a perfect map. We will run fewer
routes, more often.
• Fix It First – Maintenance comes before expansion. A broken escalator costs more
riders than a missing station.
• Pay As We Go – We will fund projects from the transit levy rather than new debt.
• Measure Everything – Every project publishes its ridership and travel-time results
every quarter.4
2. Districts
A. Downtown: Make Room for Buses
Downtown streets carry 40 percent of all bus riders. We will add bus lanes on
Harbor Avenue and Market Street, and retime signals to give buses priority.
B. Eastside: Close the Gaps
Eastside has the most car-dependent neighborhoods. We will connect the five bike
lanes that end at the rail line and add two cross-town routes.
C. Waterfront: Shift the Freight
Truck traffic on the waterfront doubled since 2018. We will move freight deliveries
to night hours and build a consolidation center near the port, freeing the curb for
loading and short-term parking during the day.
